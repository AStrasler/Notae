The Notae of Anatomy & Physiology I

Five study figures, each packing a chapter into a single picture, the way the medieval notae packed a whole art into one figure meant to be studied by looking.

How to use these

Start at the center and read outward, one ring to a breath. When the rings are done, follow each red line out to its corner and read that. Close with the two scrolls, top then bottom. Once you know a nota, stop reading and just look at it: try to recall each ring before your eyes reach it.

About the form

The notae come from the Ars Notoria, a thirteenth-century Latin text that promised mastery of the liberal arts through prayer and long contemplation of ornate figures. Critics called it demonic. The simpler explanation is that a dense, carefully ordered picture is easy for the mind to hold. These five keep the old form and put the actual material inside it.

If your professor asks what this is, tell them: it’s a modern nota, a medieval contemplative study form rebuilt for anatomy. Reading is labor. This just makes the labor visible.
